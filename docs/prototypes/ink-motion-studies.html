Three ways to put a moving graphic on the page

The site animates text arriving. None of that produces a graphic — there is no object on screen whose job is to move. These are three candidates for that object, drawn in the site's own ink on its own paper, so the editorial page stays intact. Watch them, pick one.

Reduce Motion is switched on in your system settings, so each study is showing a single frozen frame. That same setting is why the live site shows you no motion at all.

A

Idea to product

One continuous line, drawn live. It wanders — searching, uncertain — then resolves into the outline of a phone and holds. It is the sentence at the top of your site, said as a drawing rather than as words.

Where it sitsThe opening, beside the h1
CharacterDeliberate, once per visit
Cost~2 kB, canvas 2D
anime.jsNot needed
B

Contour field

Topographic hairlines that never stop moving — an engraving that breathes. It gives the page a living surface without ever asking to be looked at, and it is the only one of the three that is still moving while you read.

Where it sitsFull-bleed band between sections
CharacterAmbient, continuous
Cost~1.5 kB, canvas 2D
anime.jsNot needed
C

The split

A bill divides itself, over and over, into shares that never come out even. This one is not abstract: it is what xBill does, drawn as a diagram. The accent marks the share you owe.

Where it sitsThe xBill product section
CharacterExplanatory, loops
Cost~2.5 kB, canvas 2D
anime.jsNot needed

How to choose

A is a statement and is over in eight seconds. B is atmosphere and never stops. C earns its place by explaining a product rather than decorating a page. They are not exclusive — A and C could both ship, in different sections.

All three are canvas 2D drawn by hand, so whichever you pick, the 18 kB anime.js dependency currently in the live bundle can be removed. Each holds a still resting state under Reduce Motion, the way the rest of the site does.
